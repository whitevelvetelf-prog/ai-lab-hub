## First, the honest part: what AI can't do

In January 2024 the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) issued an advisory with a telling title: ["AI Won't Turn Trading Bots into Money Machines"](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html). The key message is simple: AI cannot predict the future or sudden market changes. Scammers advertise AI algorithms that supposedly deliver "tens of thousands of percent" in returns or 100% winning trades, and the CFTC calls exactly these promises a red flag. The same advisory tells the story of Mirror Trading International: a bot "guaranteeing 10% a month" turned out to be a Ponzi scheme in which more than 23,000 people lost over $1.7 billion in bitcoin.

It can be subtler, too. In March 2024 the SEC [fined two investment advisers $400,000](https://www.aibusiness.com/responsible-ai/sec-fines-advisors-400k-for-false-statements-regarding-ai-use) for false statements about their use of AI. This is called "AI washing": calling a product AI-powered when there is no real AI inside. In its [May 2024 statement](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-05/ESMA35-335435667-5924__Public_Statement_on_AI_and_investment_services.pdf), the European regulator ESMA lists among the risks of AI in investing algorithmic bias, poor data quality, opaque decisions and overreliance on AI by both firms and clients.

So rule number one: **AI is an analyst and an assistant, not a magic button.** The decisions and responsibility for your money stay with you.

## A strategy without code: describe it, test it on history

The most interesting thing AI has changed in trading is the path from an idea to a tested strategy. It used to require programming; now describing the idea in words is enough.

- **[Composer](https://ailabhub-directory.com/product.php?id=3040)** (now part of SoFi): you describe your goals, strategy and risk tolerance, the AI editor builds the algorithm, and you can backtest and run it. The base plan costs $0 a month.
- **[WealthLab](https://ailabhub-directory.com/product.php?id=3087)** lets you describe an idea in plain English, backtest it on a portfolio using real index constituent changes and refine it with Claude.
- **[QuantConnect](https://ailabhub-directory.com/product.php?id=3088)** takes a strategy from idea to live trading on the open-source LEAN engine, with an AI assistant at every stage.
- **[Option Alpha](https://ailabhub-directory.com/product.php?id=3067)**: no-code options bots. Free if you connect a TradeStation, tastytrade or Tradier account.

Keep in mind: **a good backtest is not the same as profit.** It is easy to fit a strategy to the past, and it will fall apart in the real market. Test it on periods it never saw (walk-forward) and start with paper trading.

## ChatGPT and Claude as a trading tutor

General chatbots do not know where the price will go tomorrow, but they are good at explaining, teaching and helping you think. Here are prompts that are genuinely useful:

- "Explain in simple words what a put option is and when it is used to protect a portfolio."
- "Here is my trading plan [paste]. Find its weak points and the risks I haven't thought about."
- "Compare the tax consequences of dividends and capital gains for an investor in my country buying US stocks through a foreign broker. What questions should I ask a tax adviser?"
- "Here is a conference call transcript [paste]. Pull out what management said about margins, debt and guidance."

What not to do: don't ask "which stock should I buy to make money," and don't trust specific numbers without checking. [Perplexity](https://ailabhub-directory.com/product.php?id=65) is handy because it shows its sources, which makes facts easier to verify.

## Red flags of "AI trading"

- Guaranteed or fixed returns ("10% a month").
- Asking you to send money to the "platform's" account instead of opening an account in your own name with a licensed broker.
- Only screenshots of profits and testimonials, no verified statistics.
- Pressure and urgency: "limited spots," "offer ends today."
- They contact you first on Telegram, Instagram or dating sites.
- They cannot explain what their "AI" actually does.

If you see even one of these, stop.
