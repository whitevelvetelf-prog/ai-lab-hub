# Midjourney alternatives: free and cheaper AI image generators

[Midjourney](product:Midjourney) is still seen as the benchmark for AI image aesthetics: a recognisable "cinematic" look, great lighting and a strong artistic feel. But it has two drawbacks that send people looking for a replacement. First, **you can't try it for free on the website or in Discord**: you need a subscription. Second, generating a lot without waiting means paying for the pricier tiers.

The good news: in 2026 there are dozens of generators that are completely free, give daily credits or cost two to three times less. And if you have a computer with a graphics card, you can generate with no subscription at all, offline.

This article collects options for any budget: from "zero money and no sign-up" to professional tools that beat Midjourney at specific tasks.

> **Important.** We checked prices and limits against the official websites on 29 October 2026. Free tiers of image generators change often, sometimes monthly. Check the current plan on the service's website before paying.

## What Midjourney actually costs

To compare fairly, first here is what you get for your money with Midjourney (according to its official help centre):

| Plan | Monthly price | Paid yearly | What matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $10 | $8/mo ($96/yr) | ~3.3 hours of "fast" GPU time (200 minutes), no unlimited mode |
| Standard | $30 | $24/mo | 15 fast hours + unlimited images in Relax mode |
| Pro | $60 | $48/mo | 30 hours, Stealth Mode (private images) |
| Mega | $120 | $96/mo | 60 hours, Stealth Mode |

A few more details worth knowing:

- **There is no free trial on the website or in Discord.** A limited trial exists only in the niji·journey mobile app (iOS and Android), a separate product tuned for anime style.
- **Your images are public** unless you are on Pro or Mega: only Stealth Mode hides them from the gallery.
- **Companies earning more than $1 million a year** need Pro or Mega for commercial use.

So the realistic "comfortable" minimum for an active user is $30 a month. That's what we'll compare against.

## How we chose the alternatives

We looked at four things:

1. **Can you use it for free**, and how generous are the limits.
2. **Quality and style**: can you get a "Midjourney-level" result.
3. **Image rights**: can you use the result commercially on the free plan.
4. **Barrier to entry**: sign-up, language, complexity of settings.

All links below lead to product cards in our catalog with a description, pricing and a link to the official website. The full list of generators is in the ["Image generation"](subcategory:image-generation) subcategory.

## Part 1. Completely free generators

If your budget is zero, start here. Quality varies, but for ideas, sketches, avatars, social media illustrations and plain fun these services are often enough.

### Bing Image Creator and Microsoft Designer

[Bing Image Creator](product:Bing Image Creator) is Microsoft's image generator right inside Bing search. All you need is a Microsoft account (the same one you use for Outlook or Xbox). Write a description and get several variants. Fast generations are limited; after that you can wait longer or earn bonuses through Microsoft Rewards.

[Microsoft Designer](product:Microsoft Designer) builds on the same idea but focuses on design: cards, invitations, social posts, stickers. Handy when you need a ready layout rather than "just a picture".

**Best for:** people who want decent quality for free and already have a Microsoft account.

### Perchance: no sign-up, no limits

[Perchance AI Image Generator](product:Perchance AI Image Generator) is the simplest option of all: open the page, type a prompt, pick a style and generate. The site states plainly that the generator is **free, with no sign-up and no cap on generations**. Quality is below top paid models, but for quick ideas it's probably the easiest way in.

### Raphael AI and Craiyon

[Raphael AI](product:Raphael AI) also lets you generate for free without logging in. Free images carry a watermark, while paid plans add access to popular third-party models, clean outputs and commercial use.

[Craiyon](product:Craiyon) (formerly DALL·E mini) is one of the oldest free generators. The free plan is limited and watermarked, but it has separate modes for photos, anime, logos, colouring pages and posters, plus background removal and image-to-SVG.

### Image generation in chatbots

If you already use an AI assistant, check whether it can draw. [ChatGPT](product:ChatGPT), [Google Gemini](product:Google Gemini), [Meta AI](product:Meta AI), [Grok](product:Grok) and [Qwen Chat](product:Qwen Chat) can create and edit images right in the conversation. Their free limits change, but a few pictures a day is usually fine.

The advantage of chatbots is **editing with words**: "make the background lighter", "remove the person on the right", "the same character in winter clothes". For many everyday tasks that is more convenient than Midjourney with its parameters.

## Part 2. Free plans with daily credits

These services have paid tiers, but the free plan here isn't a "one-day sample": you can use it regularly.

### Leonardo AI: 150 tokens every day

[Leonardo AI](product:Leonardo AI) is one of the closest to Midjourney in quality and features. The free plan gives **150 fast tokens a day**, but the model is chosen automatically and results are public. The paid Essential plan **starts at $12 a month**: access to 80+ models, private results and training your own models.

**Best for:** illustrators, game designers and content creators who need characters, concept art and a consistent style.

### Adobe Firefly: free daily generations

[Adobe Firefly](product:Adobe Firefly) lets you generate **without a plan, with a daily limit** that refreshes every day. Besides Adobe's own models, popular third-party models are available too. Paid plans **start at $11.99 a month**; the price may vary by country.

Firefly's trump card is integration with Photoshop, Illustrator and [Adobe Express](product:Adobe Express): generate and then refine in familiar apps.

### Ideogram: the best text in images

[Ideogram](product:Ideogram) is the top choice when the picture needs **legible text**: a poster, a cover, a logo, a meme, packaging. Free accounts get weekly credits (when signing in with Google, Apple or Microsoft). The paid Plus plan costs **$20 a month**, or $15 a month billed annually ($180), and includes unlimited "slow" credits.

### Magnific (formerly Freepik): 20 images a day

Freepik has rebranded as Magnific, but the image generator remains: [Magnific (Freepik)](product:Magnific) gives **20 free images a day with no card required**. A nice feature: more than 15 models from different makers live here, and the service can pick the best one for your prompt. Handy for comparing models without signing up for each service separately.

### Krea: 100 units a day and real-time generation

[Krea](product:Krea) is known for "live" generation: draw or type and the image changes in real time. Free: **100 units a day**, no card. Paid plans **start at $5.25 a month** billed annually, making Krea one of the cheapest ways to access several strong models.

### Playground AI: a commercial licence even for free

[Playground AI](product:Playground AI) today looks more like an AI design editor with templates. The free plan allows **up to 10 images every 3 hours** and, unusually, comes with a **commercial licence**. Pro is $15 a month or $12 a month billed annually.

### Recraft: for vectors, logos and brand identity

[Recraft](product:Recraft) specialises in design: vector graphics, icons, logos and illustrations in a consistent style. The free plan is modest, 3 generations per style per day, and importantly **images from the free plan are public and not licensed for commercial use**. Basic costs **$10 a month** billed annually ($120) and gives 1,000 credits and full commercial rights.

### NightCafe: credits for activity

[NightCafe](product:NightCafe) is a generator with a community, daily challenges and contests. Free "relax" credits are earned by logging in daily, entering challenges, earning badges and keeping streaks. It suits people who want not only to generate but also to show their work.

### Other services worth knowing

- [Canva](product:Canva): image generation built into the most popular online editor, so you can drop the picture straight into a presentation or post.
- [Artbreeder](product:Artbreeder): "breeding" images and editing their "genes" (age, colours, features). A favourite of writers and tabletop authors for creating characters. Some features are free.
- [Dreamina](product:Dreamina): an image and video generator from CapCut.
- [OpenArt](product:OpenArt), [getimg.ai](product:getimg.ai), [SeaArt AI](product:SeaArt AI), [StarryAI](product:StarryAI): platforms with many models and ready-made styles, handy for trying different models in one place.
- [Pixlr](product:Pixlr): an online photo editor with generation and AI editing tools.

## Part 3. Offline: generate on your own computer with no subscription

This is the most interesting alternative for people who need **lots** of images. You install an app once, download an open model and generate as much as you like, with no queues, no limits, no third-party platform moderation and without sending your prompts to the cloud. You only pay for electricity.

What you need: a computer with a graphics card (ideally NVIDIA with 6–8 GB of VRAM or more) or a Mac with Apple Silicon. We've collected all such apps in a separate ["Local image generators"](subcategory:local-image-generation) subcategory.

### Models: what you actually run

The app is just a "shell". The model does the generating, and the most popular open families are [Stable Diffusion](product:Stable Diffusion) from Stability AI and [FLUX.1](product:FLUX.1) from Black Forest Labs. Some models have open weights, but **licences differ**: some allow commercial use, some don't. Check the licence of the specific model.

Community models, styles and fine-tunes (LoRA) can be found on [Civitai](product:Civitai) and [Hugging Face](product:Hugging Face).

### Fooocus: "Midjourney on your PC"

The authors of [Fooocus](product:Fooocus) explicitly designed it as an offline counterpart to online generators like Midjourney: minimal settings, write a prompt and get a quality picture. Installation takes a few clicks; the requirement is an NVIDIA GPU with **4 GB** or more. It runs Stable Diffusion XL models.

A caveat: the project is now in long-term support with bug fixes only, and new models aren't being added. Also, **the only official source of Fooocus is its GitHub repository**. According to the authors, sites with names like fooocus.com or fooocus.ai are fake.

### Forge and AUTOMATIC1111: the enthusiast classics

[Stable Diffusion WebUI](product:Stable Diffusion WebUI) by AUTOMATIC1111 is the best-known interface for local generation: txt2img, img2img, inpainting, outpainting, upscaling and a huge number of community extensions. But the original project is now updated rarely.

That's why many people move to [Stable Diffusion WebUI Forge](product:Stable Diffusion WebUI Forge) from the author of Fooocus: the same interface, but better memory management, faster generation and FLUX support in compressed formats (NF4, GGUF), so heavier models run even on a mid-range GPU.

### ComfyUI: maximum flexibility

[ComfyUI](product:ComfyUI) is a node-based interface: you build the generation process from blocks, like a construction kit. The learning curve is steeper, but this is where support for new models appears first, and the whole community shares ready-made workflows. If you plan to generate seriously, you'll end up here sooner or later.

### InvokeAI: a studio for artists

[InvokeAI](product:InvokeAI) has the most convenient canvas (Unified Canvas) for painting, inpainting and outpainting, plus node workflows. Its Apache-2.0 licence allows commercial use of the app itself. It installs via a launcher.

### DiffusionBee and Draw Things: no command line needed

[DiffusionBee](product:DiffusionBee) is a regular app with an installer for macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Windows: generation, generative fill, upscaling and even training your own models, all locally with no technical setup.

[Draw Things](product:Draw Things) brings local generation to iPhone, iPad and Mac: images are created right on the device.

### Krita AI Diffusion: for people who draw themselves

[Krita AI Diffusion](product:Krita AI Diffusion) is a free plugin for the Krita painting app. Here AI is an artist's tool, not a replacement: generate within a selection, refine your sketch, ControlNet from a pose or line art, and Live Painting that interprets the canvas in real time. Locally an NVIDIA GPU with 6 GB or more is recommended, and cloud generation is available to get started.

## What to choose: a quick cheat sheet

- **I want to try for free right now**: [Perchance AI Image Generator](product:Perchance AI Image Generator) with no sign-up, or [Bing Image Creator](product:Bing Image Creator) with a Microsoft account.
- **I need Midjourney-like quality for less**: [Leonardo AI](product:Leonardo AI) or [Krea](product:Krea); to compare models, [Magnific (Freepik)](product:Magnific).
- **I need text in the image (posters, covers, memes)**: [Ideogram](product:Ideogram).
- **Logos, icons, vectors**: [Recraft](product:Recraft) (but commercial use needs a paid plan).
- **I work in Photoshop or Illustrator**: [Adobe Firefly](product:Adobe Firefly).
- **I want to edit with words**: [ChatGPT](product:ChatGPT) or [Google Gemini](product:Google Gemini).
- **I need a thousand pictures a month and have a GPU**: [Fooocus](product:Fooocus) to start, then [Stable Diffusion WebUI Forge](product:Stable Diffusion WebUI Forge) or [ComfyUI](product:ComfyUI).
- **I have a Mac or iPhone**: [DiffusionBee](product:DiffusionBee) or [Draw Things](product:Draw Things).
- **I'm an artist and draw myself**: [Krita AI Diffusion](product:Krita AI Diffusion) or [InvokeAI](product:InvokeAI).

## What to check before you start

- **Image rights.** On free plans results are often public, watermarked or without commercial rights. If the picture is going into an ad, onto packaging or up for sale, read the plan's terms.
- **Privacy.** Whatever you upload to an online generator (photos, sketches, prompts) is stored on someone else's servers and may appear in a public gallery. For confidential projects, local generation is better.
- **Real people and other people's styles.** Don't generate images of real people without their consent, and don't pass off generated images as real photos. Copying recognisable characters and logos may infringe copyright.
- **Fake websites.** Popular open-source apps (Fooocus, ComfyUI and others) have impostor sites. Download only from the official repository or website.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Midjourney have a free version?**
No. There's no trial on the website or in Discord; a limited trial exists only in the niji·journey mobile app. The cheapest plan is Basic at $10 a month.

**Which generator is closest to Midjourney in quality?**
For artistic style people most often compare [Leonardo AI](product:Leonardo AI), [Krea](product:Krea) and the modern models in [Adobe Firefly](product:Adobe Firefly) and [Magnific (Freepik)](product:Magnific). There's no clear winner: run the same prompt in 2–3 services.

**Can I generate for free with no limits?**
Yes, in two ways: [Perchance AI Image Generator](product:Perchance AI Image Generator) online (with lower quality), or local apps like [Fooocus](product:Fooocus) and [ComfyUI](product:ComfyUI) on your own computer with a GPU.

**What graphics card do I need for offline generation?**
Fooocus runs from 4 GB of NVIDIA VRAM; Krita AI Diffusion recommends 6 GB or more. Heavier models like FLUX prefer 8–12 GB or more, but Forge helps run them on weaker cards thanks to compressed formats.

**Can I use free images commercially?**
It depends on the service. For example, [Playground AI](product:Playground AI) gives a commercial licence even on the free plan, while free images from [Recraft](product:Recraft) can't be used commercially. Always check the plan's terms.

**Do generators understand prompts in languages other than English?**
Many services, such as [Bing Image Creator](product:Bing Image Creator) and chatbots, accept prompts in various languages. But for most models an English prompt still gives a more precise result, so translate your description if in doubt.

## Summary

Midjourney is still a strong tool, but it's long since stopped being the only one. For most tasks the free plan of [Leonardo AI](product:Leonardo AI), [Adobe Firefly](product:Adobe Firefly) or [Magnific (Freepik)](product:Magnific) is enough; [Ideogram](product:Ideogram) is better for text in images and [Recraft](product:Recraft) for vectors. And if you need lots of pictures and have a GPU, local apps like [Fooocus](product:Fooocus) or [ComfyUI](product:ComfyUI) remove the subscription question altogether.

The best way to choose is to take one of your typical prompts and run it through 3–4 services from this article. In half an hour it'll be clear which style suits you and what's actually worth paying for.
