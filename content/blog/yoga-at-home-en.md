# Yoga at home: apps, online studios and a beginner plan

You do not need a studio near your home or its timetable to practise yoga. A mat, two square metres of floor and 15–30 minutes are enough. Apps and online studios provide the rest: the pose sequence, cues, pace and motivation. Some are free, others cost about as much per month as one or two studio classes.

This article explains where to start, which yoga style to pick, how apps differ from online studios, how to avoid injury and where AI can help. All services from the article are in the new [Yoga](subcategory:yoga) subcategory of the Sports section.

> **Important.** Yoga is physical exercise. If you are pregnant, have back or joint problems, high blood pressure, glaucoma, recent surgery or an injury, talk to a doctor first. Practice should not cause sharp pain: if it hurts, come out of the pose. We checked prices and terms against official websites on November 5, 2026; they can change.

## Which yoga style to choose

Style names often scare beginners, but the idea is simple:

- **Hatha**: slow pace, poses held for several breaths, lots of explanation. The best place to start.
- **Vinyasa (flow)**: poses flow into each other in rhythm with the breath. More dynamic and warming.
- **Ashtanga**: a fixed sequence of poses done the same way every time. Intense and disciplined; better to come to it with a foundation.
- **Yin yoga**: passive floor poses held for 3–5 minutes, gentle stretching and relaxation. Good in the evening.
- **Restorative yoga**: even gentler, with cushions and blankets as props. For rest and stress relief.
- **Kundalini**: combines movement, breathing exercises, mantras and meditation.
- **Prenatal yoga**: practice adapted for pregnancy (only after your doctor agrees).

For the first month, hatha or slow beginner vinyasa is enough. Add yin or restorative yoga in the evening.

## Apps and online studios: what is the difference

**Practice generators.** [Down Dog](product:Down Dog) does not play pre-recorded videos: every time it builds a new sequence for your settings, including length, level, body focus, pace, voice and music. It has prenatal yoga as well as separate Pilates, HIIT, barre and meditation practices. Practices can be downloaded for offline use and cast to a TV, and the app works in 13 languages. [Pocket Yoga](product:Pocket Yoga) for iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple Watch offers ready practices, a pose dictionary and practices from community teachers.

**Video studios.** These are libraries of recorded classes by real teachers. [Glo](product:Glo) has 12,000+ yoga, Pilates, meditation and breathwork classes. [Yoga Anytime](product:Yoga Anytime) covers 19+ styles from 120+ teachers, including thousands of hatha and vinyasa classes, and has the longest trial at 15 days. [Gaia](product:Gaia) has a yoga section with practices, series and a pose library.

**Going deeper.** Besides 3,000+ classes, [Yoga International](product:Yoga International) offers 300+ courses on philosophy, anatomy and yoga therapy that count as Yoga Alliance continuing education credits for teachers. [Omstars](product:Omstars) was founded by Ashtanga teacher Kino MacGregor: 5,000+ classes, multi-week courses, daily live classes and yin yoga teacher training.

**A free start.** [Yoga With Adriene](product:Yoga With Adriene) is a YouTube channel with 12M+ subscribers, beginner foundations and yearly 30-day journeys that make a regular practice easy to build. The paid Find What Feels Good membership adds 700+ ad-free videos. Yoga is also part of general fitness services: [Peloton App](product:Peloton App) (unlimited yoga on the App One plan, 30 days free), [Apple Fitness+](product:Apple Fitness+) and [Nike Training Club](product:Nike Training Club).

**Tradition.** [Isha Hatha Yoga](product:Isha Hatha Yoga) offers classical hatha yoga programs from the Isha Foundation, led by trained teachers; the site helps you find the nearest program in your country. These are in-person sessions, but you get hands-on technique you can then continue at home.

## How to practise safely

- **Start with short sessions.** 15–20 minutes 3–4 times a week does more than an hour once a week.
- **Pain means stop.** A stretching sensation is normal. Sharp, stabbing pain or pain in a joint is not.
- **Don't chase the picture.** Bend your knees in forward folds and use blocks, a strap or books as props.
- **Be careful with inversions.** Do headstands and shoulder stands only after preparation, ideally with a teacher.
- **Breathe.** If your breath gets ragged or you hold it, the pose is too much right now; make it easier.
- **Place your phone or laptop to the side.** Then you won't twist your neck in forward folds.
- **Don't practise right after eating.** Wait 1.5–2 hours.

## A plan for the first 4 weeks

| Week | What to do | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 sessions of 15–20 minutes: basics, foundational poses, breathing | [Yoga With Adriene](product:Yoga With Adriene) (beginner foundations) or [Down Dog](product:Down Dog) at beginner level |
| 2 | 3–4 sessions of 20 minutes, one of them a gentle evening practice | [Down Dog](product:Down Dog), [Glo](product:Glo) |
| 3 | 4 sessions of 20–30 minutes; try slow vinyasa and yin yoga | [Yoga Anytime](product:Yoga Anytime), [Glo](product:Glo) |
| 4 | 4 sessions, one focused on back or hips; pick 1–2 favourite styles | whichever service you liked during its trial |

Note how you feel after sessions: flexibility, sleep, mood. After a month you will see what works for you.

## Where AI helps

- **Personal sequences.** [Down Dog](product:Down Dog) generates a new practice every time for your settings, so sessions don't repeat or get boring.
- **Plans and explanations.** Chat assistants such as [ChatGPT](product:ChatGPT) or [Claude](product:Claude) can draft a weekly plan around your schedule, explain a pose or style name and suggest how to make a pose easier. But AI cannot see your body or know your medical history: medical questions go to a doctor and advanced poses to a real teacher.
- **Camera pose analysis.** Apps are appearing that use the phone camera to judge whether you are in a pose correctly. The technology is still imprecise and depends on lighting, camera angle and clothing, so treat such cues as guidance, not truth.

For calm and sleep, meditation pairs well with yoga: see [Insight Timer](product:Insight Timer), [Headspace](product:Headspace) or [Calm](product:Calm), and the article [AI for psychology and mental health](blog-post.php?slug=ai-for-psychology-and-mental-health&hl=en).

## FAQ

**Can I learn yoga from apps alone?**
The basics, yes; many people start that way. But one or two sessions with a live teacher early on help you get the foundational poses right and see your mistakes.

**What equipment do I need?**
A non-slip mat. Blocks and a strap are useful, but at first books and a bathrobe belt will do. For restorative yoga you'll need cushions and a blanket.

**How often should I practise?**
Beginners: 3–4 times a week for 15–30 minutes. Consistency matters more than length.

**Will yoga help my back?**
Many people feel better with gentle regular practice. But back pain has different causes, so find out yours from a doctor first and choose gentle styles.

**Is free enough?**
Absolutely. The free [Yoga With Adriene](product:Yoga With Adriene) videos are enough for months of practice. Paid services add variety, programs, a convenient app and no ads.
